Setup

No bundler is needed. An import map in the page's <head> tells the browser where three lives: 

<script type="importmap">
{
  "imports": {
    "three": "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.170.0/build/three.module.js",
    "three/addons/": "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.170.0/examples/jsm/"
  }
}
</script>
<script type="module">
  import * as THREE from 'three';
</script>

ES modules don't load from file://, so serve the folder: npx serve threejs or python3 -m http.server -d threejs.

1. Hello, Cube

Every three.js program has a Scene (the world), a Camera (the eye) and a Renderer (draws the scene from the camera onto a canvas). Visible things are Meshes: a geometry (shape) plus a material (look).

2. Geometries & Materials

From here on, lessons use a tiny setup() helper (lessons/setup.js) that does the Lesson 1 boilerplate for us. Note how MeshStandardMaterial options like roughness, metalness, flatShading and wireframe change the look of each shape.

3. Lights & Shadows

Common lights: AmbientLight / HemisphereLight (soft fill), DirectionalLight (sun), PointLight (bulb) and SpotLight. Shadows are opt-in, in three places.

4. Animation & the Scene Graph

Nesting objects with parent.add(child) builds a hierarchy. Transforms stack up, so a moon orbiting a planet orbiting a sun needs only three simple rotations.

5. Camera Controls

Extras such as controls, loaders and post-processing live in three/addons/. Try it: drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan.
